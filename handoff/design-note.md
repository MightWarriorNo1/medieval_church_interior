# Nave — design note

The church is one parametric nave, built in metres and regenerated when the bay, width, or crown slider moves. The hour slider only moves the sun. Default is seven bays of 6.6 m, a 12 m nave, and a crown at 27.2 m, which sits in the 25–30 m range the brief asked for on a nave this wide.

Piers, arches, vaults, triforium, clerestory, and the two end walls are generated in world space and merged into a few meshes. Capitals and bases are lathes. The nave vault is a ribbed web with a boss at each bay, and the aisles have their own smaller bosses. Nothing is a downloaded model. Stone and wood are shaders that read position in the world, so the ashlar and the grain do not depend on UVs. Glass is one shared shader. Each window gets its own pattern, painted into a canvas atlas when the church is built, and that pattern stays put. Changing the hour changes which windows are lit and where the coloured patches fall, not the leading.

The sun is a directional light. Noon comes from the south. Dawn and late afternoon go warm. Coloured light on the floor is a cheap splash from the same window list, with a simpler version in Fast mode. Candles are small meshes with a capped set of point lights, altar first, and Fast mode keeps only those. Walk mode is eye height, WASD, and pointer lock. Piers, pews, the sanctuary steps, and the west portal stop you. Capitals carry a ring of crockets made from three boxes. Bosses carry a smaller cluster of the same leaf. A short bloom pass runs after the scene and then the same ACES tone mapping as before, so only the bright glass and the altar pick up a halo.

A few things were built and then taken out or moved. Wood and cloth were proved with test boxes at the west door, then the boxes were removed once the pews, frontal, and linen were in. Candle lights first sat on the altar top. From the west door that face is edge-on, so the pool never showed. They now sit just west of the frontal, which is the face you actually see. The first crocket curled back into the capital, and the first boss cluster sat inside the sphere. Both were turned outward until they read as leaves. The plain lathe of the capital is still there under the ring.

Another week would go into the carving and the light, not more architecture. The crockets are still boxes, and they read at the capital more clearly than they do on a boss twenty-seven metres up. A modelled leaf, with a vein and a curl that is not three cubes, would do more for the close view than another moulding. The glass could take thicker leading and a little dirt in the corners. The dust in the shafts is there, and it could pick up the window colour instead of staying grey. The Fast path already drops the extra candle lights. What is still worth a sitting is a measured hour on the Surface at 1920×1280, pixel ratio 1.5, and cutting bloom resolution only if that machine misses 60.
